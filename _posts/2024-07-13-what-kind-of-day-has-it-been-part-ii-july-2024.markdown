So in the last blog post, I talked about how I barely fought off my final year. That was the first time I actually thought that maybe, just maybe, I couldn't survive as there were too many battles that were too difficult by themselves to fight off at the same time - No work authorization leading to no job interviews whatsoever; Difficult 4k Physics modules and difficult Physics final year thesis; Skyrocketing rental prices; NUS accommodation student leader tried to screw me; etc. So far in my life, I have had 3 major periods of struggles: one during my time at Dunman High, one after an elongated half-self-imposed lockdown due to COVID, as well as this one. This particular period of struggle is probably a close second in terms of severity.

I was quite inspired by Rilke's wise words: "Wer spricht von Siegen? berstehn ist alles" which roughly translates to "Who speaks of victory? Endurance is everything." I think at the time, I already kind of made my peace with it - thinking: Yep there is no way I am coming out of this in one piece. I will just go down swinging and keep resisting and see what's the best-case scenario I could get. In the end, I think I did mostly okay in the end: I handled the accommodation and rental situations; I managed to land a reasonable job offer that had compensation comparable to Citadel and does C++17/Python3 development day-to-day (so basically something I was gunning for during my late undergraduate years); I got an A- in my physics thesis and extremely narrowly passed Math Method 3 (Honestly it was quite bad, especially for my thesis - and not in terms of grade. I don't care about the grade itself per se, because it does not make a difference whether I got A+ or B-, I would graduate with a second upper either way. **I regret simply because I know I was more than capable of producing a research with a much higher quality and I feel I have let down my supervisor and the part of me so passionate about physics, especially since I know I could have done a better job and my supervisor knows it too. It is one thing if I was only capable of bringing a B game and ended up bringing a B game. This case felt more like I was capable of bringing an A game, people expected me to produce a B game at least and then I simply showed up with a C game, kind of like Lebron in the 2011 Finals if you know what I am talking about. That's why it is just so regrettable. I wanted to do more and I could do more, but I made a conscious choice to spend limited time and effort on it so I could prioritize things like job search. I don't regret this choice itself as it is what the situation called for, but I am sorry that my physics thesis was far from my best effort)**. So in the end I suppose in terms of the final outcome with everything considered, I guess it was fine.

So that marked the end of my undergraduate studies, and also possibly the end of my academic pursuit. I am not sure whether I would go for a Master's or a PhD. In China, where I come from, there is this general sentiment that degree qualifications matter - PhD > Master's > Bachelor's. I simply do not agree at all. I think it is simply a matter of choice. I think lots of undergraduates are extremely talented and a Bachelor's degree already more than suffices. They choose to do things they deem more important than burying themselves in research for 5 years which they may not even like in the first place. Now, I have known some people who are really passionate about research and really stick to the academic track - and that is fine. In fact, I respect them for it (Like this great senior of mine, Ruomu. He is quite possibly the top 3 or top 5 most talented people I have ever known in my life. I have a hell lot of respect for him and what he does and the determination with which he does it. He is just so talented and gifted and determined to stick to this research track. He does [distributed algorithms research](https://dblp.org/pid/227/7172.html) mostly. But at the same time, I also know a bunch of people who don't know what they are doing and have no purpose at all, simply mindlessly going for Master's or even PhDs and coming out even more clueless than when they got in. So I think this is more of a matter of a choice really. I don't think PhDs are necessarily superior to Bachelor degree holders at all and I definitely don't think Master's are superior either - I mean, come on, the only people who do Masters are people who try to boost their resume, or try to switch careers, or try to spend their non-competes. You don't actually learn things in Masters. For people who are passionate about PhDs, they will skip Master's and do PhDs directly. And of course, school matters too, and generally speaking, I would think a school's PhDs/Master's are probably inferior to that school's Bachelor degree holders. Of course there could be edge cases like bond issues and whatnot, but generally speaking, if a student is pursuing PhD/Master's at a second-tier university, then chances are his Bachelor's study was probably done at a third-tier university or worse. Surely I would consider his portfolio inferior to an undergraduate at the same university.

So circling back, as of today, I have been working for approximately over a year now. And I would say it is quite different from what I imagined. I mean, I didn't come into the workforce as a complete newbie. I have done 3 internships - including one at Goldman and one at Citadel. But it is definitely very different from what I expected. There were new struggles (albeit on a much smaller scale - especially compared to the one I suffered in my final year), and it took me a while to adjust. I think this process has also made me more empathetic in some sense - because I know some of my peers back in my freshman year at NUS were really lost and didn't know what they were doing. They lost their sense of purpose and didn't know what to do with their university life. I mean they still got good GPAs like 4.9x/5.00, but they were mostly disoriented and lost themselves. I didn't have that. I quickly figured out and I knew exactly where I was going and what I was doing - and I can't take all the credits as I have had great seniors and friends help me and guide me along the way. But yeah, I had a purpose and I was quite clear on what I intended to accomplish at the end of the day. But now, after entering the workforce, that similar loss of sense of meaning has struck me, so there was a bit of struggle there. So this experience has made me more empathetic towards what others might have experienced 5 years ago.
