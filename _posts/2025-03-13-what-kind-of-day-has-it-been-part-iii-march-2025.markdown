It has been a while since my last update again. In the previous parts of the blog I focused mostly on the university part of my life, and there is a good reason for reminiscing that part of my life - it had clearly defined milestones and I knew exactly where I was and where I was going. After having started working full-time, I have not quite been able to find my footing. Even now, coming up to 2 years of full-time work, the situation has not quite improved observably compared to one year ago.

It has not been easy to stay motivated day-to-day, when getting work done faster usually just means receiving more work. It is not quite as straightforward to just go all out for a change, especially when I have been presented with 2 sides of the story. You see, previously the concept was straightforward: Grind for the 'better' companies and get higher compensation, and I experienced through that in my undergrad. I worked for a fintech startup with 30 employees in the first summer, then went for Goldman and got 3x compensation, then went for Citadel and got 2x compensation. With rising compensation, my workload increased accordingly. At Citadel I basically spent most - if not all - of my waking moment working, and essentially overclocked myself to the absolute maximum.

After graduation, I was presented with a different route, where it was possible for me to get compensated at the same level (well, could be higher actually. I don't know what they pay for their Singapore based fresh grads these days) as Citadel fresh grads, while only working maybe around half of the working hours at half of the intensity, with support for full work from home. In the meantime, I see my friends at Citadel grinding in the office until 9pm or 10pm on week days. So this stark contrast has been consistently questioning me whether my old beliefs are still valid.

Of course, right now it would not make a lot of sense for me to go back to Citadel, even if opportunities present themselves. It would be kind of crazy to work 2x hours at 2x intensity for the same pay. (I mean, for what?) But that does not mean the appeal of top-tier HFTs has been completely lost on me, where it is possible to work alongside the very best for maybe 2x the compensation. But that is a different comparison and I have put a lot of thoughts into it without a clear evaluation. I have thought a lot about it myself. I have talked to my friends and my family. I have even fed my thought processes into ChatGPT - It comes back and scolds me for having decision paralysis. So I try not to put too much thought into it these days because that seems to be going nowhere.

Nevertheless, how things transpired for me in recent years have led me to ponder: what is a good company and what is a bad company? I was raised to believe the prestigious companies are good, and obviously you would want to join them. For instance, I was raised to believe the big investment banks like Goldman and JPM are good. I was raised to believe the big techs like Google and Facebook are good. I was raised to believe Citadel, Tower Research, etc. are good. etc.

But now it is not so clear for me. Is it good when companies prioritise diversity hire over meritocracy? Is it good when companies fire people not because they did anything wrong or because they are slacking, but because of 'business needs'? Is it good when you have to work long hours because the firm culture kinda expects you to? The other day I was listening to this recording by JPM CEO talking down WFH. Apparently he has done that for quite a while. My understanding is that Goldman instituted this full Work-From-Office policy as well shortly after COVID.

I know this is a sensitive and controversial subject obviously. I have experienced both arrangements. I worked fully onsite back at Citadel, I worked kind of a hybrid arrangement when I was at Goldman, and now I do full remote. I genuinely see the merits of work onsite. I understand it is more productive. I understand it encourages communications inside the team or even outside the team. I remember back at Citadel, when I was using this tool provided by another team and there was a lack of clear documentation, I could just walk to that team's desk and talk to someone who could help me in one minute. This is obviously not quite doable in a remote work setting.

On the other hand, I think after working full-time, I genuinely start to see the merits of remote work as well. Daily commute is very painful. Many people have personal commitments in life. I mean, at the end of the day, people are working to make their lives better, so there is nothing wrong with choosing an arrangement that is less toxic for life to begin with.

So yeah, now for me, new reality has kicked in. I have realized prestigious companies do not necessarily operate in a way that is beneficial to employees' best interests. I have realized that having time to sleep, travel, and take care of my well-being is a huge advantage. I have realized that 'goodness' of companies is very very relative and working in the most prestigious firms do not necessarily translate to a better personal life. Of course, that does not mean I am going to jump to a start-up tomorrow. It just means I am starting to see career-related decisions in a new light with different rationales and metrics. I guess I am finally evolving a bit after all in that regard.
