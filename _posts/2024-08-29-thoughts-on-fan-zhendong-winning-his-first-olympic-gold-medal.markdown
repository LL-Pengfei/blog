As stereotypical as it sounds, one of my favourite sports since childhood has been table tennis. While I have been kind of on-and-off when it comes to playing table tennis, I have mostly kept track of the games and watch them as long as I can - That is, until a few years ago. I stopped watching the games ever since the broadcast camera angle was changed. For as long as I could recall, the camera was always positioned from behind one of the players, i.e. how audience views the game, basically the same way tennis is broadcast. Ever since a few years ago, it was rotated 90 degrees, so the camera is placed at the side, i.e. how referee views the game. And that is simply absurd to me. I could no longer see the spin clearly. I could no longer enjoy the game. So I don't follow it as often anymore. Recently, however, in the Paris Olympics, I am glad they have come to their senses (even if it is only temporary) and decided to go back to the better view, and naturally, as a die-hard table tennis fan, I finally watched table tennis again after taking a long break from it all these years.

I have been a huge fan of Fan Zhendong for a long time. He is quite possibly the last remaining super talent in table tennis from China. I remember watching his game back in 2014. He was just a 17-year-old kid and already went toe-to-toe with Ma Long, the GOAT of table tennis, at his prime, and the match already went down to the wire. After all these years, still no one could push against Ma Long to the extent Fan does. Fan has been the world's second best player for the past decade (and probably the third best of all time, after Ma Long and Waldner), and the only accomplishment he had yet to achieve (before Paris Olympics) was the individual Olympics gold medal.

Unfortunately the past 2 years went terribly for Fan. The China's national team decided to kind of sideline Fan and reallocate training resources to some other players (Wang Chuqin, Liu Shidong, etc.) Fan did not receive immediate medical attention when he was injuried, and was denied training partners and seconds often. He also had to endure through personal attacks and harassment both from the Internet and in real life. He was sidelined and attacked so much so that he has already decided to retire at the age of 27. 27! For god's sake. He already declared the Paris Olympics to be his 'Last Dance'on Weibo (China's version of Twitter) before the Olympics. And now, when it comes to the Olympics, his own predicament aside, what made matters worse was 2-fold: the difficult Olympics pairing drawing, and the fact that the other Chinese player is Wang Chuqin.

The drawing of Fan was quite unlucky - Wong Chun-ting in round 3, Harimoto in the quarter final, then F Lebrun or Lin Yun-ju in the semi. He is faced with too many tough opponents too early. On the other hand, in the other half of the pool, only Wang Chuqin, Truls Moregard and Hugo Calderano are serious contenders; And the reason why I consider Wang Chuqin most problematic for Fan (compared to other Chinese players, even the GOAT Ma Long himself) is that he frequently illegally hides his serves and more often than not, referee will not call him out, especially if the match is between 2 Chinese players and Fan does not complain. The stark contrast between the 2 players just make you like Fan a lot more, because his technique is just way cleaner and he obeys the rules. If Fan gets into a tough spot, he will use his signature reverse pendulum serve; If Wang gets into a tough spot, he will hide his serve so the opponent cannot see the spin.

 Knowing what he had been through, I obviously wished that he could win the Olympics, but did not have very high hope given the circumstances. Then miraculously Wang was knocked out by Moregard. People who only watch the Olympics would obviously be unfamiliar with this name, but he actually already got into the 2021 WTT Championships Finals 3 years ago (and lost 0-4 against Fan Zhendong), and he already made quite an impression with his unconventially-shaped racket and Waldner-like playing style back then. But truthfully, most Chinese table tennis fans, myself included, thought it was a random fluke and nothing too serious about him. His fundamentals were not particularly strong, and he didn't encounter any Chinese players until Fan in the Finals. So the sentiment back then was mostly he emerged as the silver medalist because of the lucky drawing. If he encountered any Chinese players like Liang Jingkun or Lin Gaoyuan (let alone Fan himself) in the first round, he would have been knocked out in the first round and no one would remember him (which is probably true). But his showing this time round definitely improved a lot compared to 3 years ago. Still, when he knocked out Wang Chuqin, I knew it was obviously bad news for the China's national team, and obviously suddenly a lot of pressure on Fan, but at the same time, I also saw a glimpse of light for Fan, because his absolutely toughest opponent has been knocked out, and now there is a great chance for Fan to actually win this thing.


 Then the game against Harimoto came. That was such a crazy game and a great demonstration of Fan's resilience and determination. Harimoto is a very strong player, and has been - on his own account - studying and preparing specifically against the Chinese players prior to the Olympics. And the game went downhill quickly for Fan, starting out with a 2-11. I mean, you don't see that very often. And then another 9-11 came and Fan was trailing 0-2 in a best-of-7 match. Fan then managed to adjust himself and tie the match with 2-2. Then in the crucial game 6, there were like 6 7 net balls and edge balls all in favour of Harimoto. It just goes to show that randomness and luck can easily decide a 11-point game and the margin of error is extremely narrow. But down from 0-2, 2-3, Fan managed to turn the whole thing around. In the last few points, Fan really just did long serves and was basically just saying, let's just go back to the basics and see whose fundamentals are stronger, and Fan prevailed.

 This actually isn't the first time Fan did something of similar nature. I think the first time I watched him doing this live was in 2018 World Team Table Tennis Championships, China vs Portugal. It was 3-0, and most people probably don't pay attention to matches like this. Portugal wasn't exactly a very strong opponent for the China's team either, respectfully. But I watched that live and I still remember 6 years later to this day. Fan was paired against Joao Geraldo, not exactly a super highly ranked player (compared to Fan himself of course). But in that match, Fan was in a lot of trouble. All his techniques were simply not working effectively. It was a best of 5 match and Fan was down 0-2. Then in game 4, he trailed 8-10. He was literally hanging in the balance, one point away from elimination, yet he refused to admit defeat and magically turned the whole thing around. Of course even if he lost, chances are China's team would likely still defeat Portugal and everything sort of stays invariant in the big picture, but that was the first time I really felt, damn, this kid is going places. Just as Coach Rudy used to say, don't ever underestimate the heart of a champion. And Fan has demonstrated this time and time again.

 After Fan defeated Harimoto, I felt he was not the same player anymore psychologically speaking. He already went through the worst. Against Felix Lebrun, it was a clean sweep. I mean, the kid is very talented and has huge potential. It is rare these days to have top pen-hold players, let alone a European one. Unfortunately, he was going up against a 'transformed' Fan Zhendong, and Fan's coach is Wang Hao, probably the greatest pen-hold master. There is nothing much Felix could do in that match. Fan was already in the zone from the get-go. (To be honest I did have higher hope for Felix. I didn't think he could actually win the match but at least I thought he would put up more of a fight. After all, he just eliminated Lin Yun-ju, who has a crazily strong backhand, possibly as strong as Fan at his prime.) Finally, Fan defeated Moregard and clinched his first individual gold medal. Moregard is playing a lot better this time than 3 years ago honestly speaking. His defense is much better, in a sense that lots of killer blows cannot finish him off. It proved to be quite difficult to actually score against him and his silver medal is surely well-deserved.

 Fan Zhendong was a man on a mission, and he won this brilliantly. Obviously as a table tennis fan I wish to see him continue playing, because he is quite literally the last remaining super talent. After Fan and Ma Long retire, there will not be a clearly dominant player anymore. Chinese players will continue to be strong of course, but not necessarily dominant. Harimoto, Lin Yun-ju, Lebrun brothers, Hugo and Morigard will prove to be tough and no Chinese player can say they have the absolute 100% confidence to beat them at the highest stage. Fan is still young. He is only 27. If he has the medical resources and training resources he need, there is no doubt he can defend his championship in LA (and potentially overtake Ma Long as the GOAT). It just feels absolutely wasteful of his talent for him to retire at this age. On the flip side, I do understand his decision. Enduring through being sidelined and being constantly attacked must have exhausted every single ounce of his strength. Hopefully Fan can find purpose and enjoyment in his life post-retirement, like trying out various cuisines, listening to Taylor Swift's songs, etc. At this point you just want to wish him well, not necessarily as a table tennis player, but as a person, with the most fundamental human decency.
