It has been nearly 2 years since my last blog post here, and it is not necessarily because I was busy (although busy I was indeed), but more because after a while, when I re-read the stuff I posted, I felt a little bit too cringe sometimes. So I not only became inactive (not saying I was very active before...), but I even marked this repo as private for a while. But now, I suppose if I simply do not update, this repo might as well be closed down for good - and it would be a shame because I do like to pen down my thoughts from time to time. So I suppose I will endeavour to keep this blog site updated as long as time permits.

A lot of things happened in the past 2 years. I think the last time I updated this, I secured a Citadel internship and I was going for it. So at that juncture, I was really sort of reaching the high point of not only my undergraduate candidature, but also the high point of my life in some sense. Little did I know one of my biggest setbacks ever was about to occur.

So in this internship, I think I performed quite well, but did not receive a return offer in the end, which was what I was aiming for and was what I expected with great certainty. I used to be this very confident person, but this result was plainly devastating. it took me a long time to recover from that. And when my confidence level dropped, part of my soul was torn away along with it. However, what's fascinating is that rather recently, I found out that apparently they have a database that keeps track of the ex-interns, and it appears their feedback of me was marked as positive. I mean, I always knew I was great and performed well during my time there, but it was a huge bummer to me and I just did not know what went wrong. I didn't know the reasoning behind the no offer at all. So this news did help me to just completely put it behind me psychologically. It served as a closure. It is kind of like in the Chinese movie Pegasus 2, the former champion Zhang Chi was disqualified in the race 5 years ago due to the missing lead seal, and he really doubted himself and couldn't compete at a high level anymore. But then when he found the lead seal later, he re-gained all the confidence he needed and broke his own record despite physical injuries.

But of course at the time I didn't know any of this. And I was also screwed over by the fact that as a foreign student (yes, I was still considered a foreigner in Singapore. I still am at the time of writing this blog), I need work pass sponsorship to get a job in Singapore. With the economic downturn, it was simply impossible to land a job. The reason is quite simple - all companies have limited quotas on Employment Pass, and it is obviously not a sound decision generally speaking to "waste" that precious quota on fresh graduates. Sure maybe you can perform better, but for most companies, there is no huge expectation of fresh graduates anyway so it is not like the local candidates can be way worse in that context. So why spend the quota on fresh graduates instead of experienced hires.

So usually, the idea in university is that you want to have some sort of upward trajectory. So for instance, you want to start interning at a startup, then to a standard decent firm, then go for the absolute ceiling. So for me I was exactly doing that. I started at a startup called Endowus (a robo-advisor), then to Goldman, then to Citadel. Normally, with this experience, the natural next step would be to go for top-tier market makers like Jump and HRT. Of course there is never a 100% guarantee that I could make these two. But at least it should be a cakewalk to go to the second tiers at least. Unfortunately for me, I applied for nearly 100 firms but barely received any interview invitations at all. I didn't just apply to the first tiers and second tiers. I applied all the way till the 18th tiers, but still in vain. I half-joked to my friends about filling out job applications at McDonalds when my friends reminded me they only hire citizens / prs as well.

Fortunately I finally landed a great offer, but it was very tough. I couldn't get anything as a fresh graduate, so I basically said F it, I am coming for the experienced hire roles too. But the difficult part is that it is not the way society works. Most companies nearly always choose years of experience over talent. The most promising one to me was this interview with Morgan Stanley, an Associate Java dev role. And I thought I had great chances there. I mean, I was at Goldman 2 years ago as a non-penultimate-year student, and I knew Java inside-out as I used it at both Goldman and Citadel. But much to my surprise that was a bust. So that's when I realized how difficult it is via this route, and the massive layoffs at the time did not exactly help - meaning the market was filled with unemployed ex-googles and ex-metas up for grabs at sub-market rate.

I guess I was quite lucky in the end. I got this "sort of" experienced hire offer from a market-making team. They interviewed 10+ candidates and I somehow landed the offer. But generally speaking this is not a reproducible pipeline. But other than this, I was fighting off a war on many fronts at the time. Landing a job offer was obviously the singularly most important battle, but there were other unfinished business too. I had to find a place to stay after graduation (which was time-consuming and the rental prices skyrocketed at the time). I had to finish my Physics Thesis on SHG in 2D materials and prepare for Viva sessions (which for people less familiar you can think of it as thesis defense). I had 3 4k Physics modules at the time which were extremely difficult. There was this advanced math methods module that simply eluded me (covering differentiable manifolds. I think I was just completely lost in Lie Algebra there), and my life was really hanging in the balance. I think it is safe to say that the profs went easy on me in the end. There was also some admin stuff going on in the on-campus accommodation which troubled me quite a bit.

So looking back, my final year in university was really a mess and can be considered an all-out war - which I did not lose but did not win either. I gave everything I could and with some luck I did manage to survive - although just barely. I was basically on tilt in the first semester after not getting a return offer and not getting interviews at all, and every aspect of my life was lit on fire in the second semester. It was the second most difficult period in my life thus far. But I guess the old saying does apply - "Wer spricht von Siegen? berstehn ist alles."
